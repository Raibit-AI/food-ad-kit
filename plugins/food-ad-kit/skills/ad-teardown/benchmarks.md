# Benchmarks: winning food ads on Meta (Nov 2026)

**Method.** I searched Meta's public Ad Library for active US video ads under 20 food terms. That returned 1,944 ads. A "likely winner" had to be both in the top 25 by impressions for its search and still running after 30+ days; an ad the advertiser had copied 3+ times also counted. 502 ads passed. I downloaded the top 60. Each one was measured on the machine (length, scene cuts, speech-to-text) and tagged by a reviewer from frame strips of the first 3 seconds and of the whole ad. 16 were off-topic (shoes, coaching, apps), leaving **44 food ads, 28 of them restaurants**.

**Caveat.** The Ad Library shows no results, so reach plus staying power is a proxy for "it works". Treat these as strong patterns, not laws.

## The numbers (restaurants, n=28; all food ads in brackets, n=44)

| Pattern | Restaurants | All food |
|---|---|---|
| Something moves in the first second | **79%** | 84% |
| A hand is in the shot (some or most of the ad) | **79%** | 84% |
| No one talks (music or ambience only) | **71%** | 50% |
| Phone-native, raw look (not studio polish) | **64%** | 64% |
| People on screen (staff, customers, creators) | 75% | 80% |
| Has an offer (price, deal, code) | 25% | 34% |
| Has call-to-action words on screen or spoken | 68% | 70% |
| Ends on a food shot / on a logo card | 29% / 21% | 27% / 23% |
| Brand name never appears in the video itself | 54% | 52% |
| Median length | **20.6 s** | 29.2 s |
| Median cuts per minute | **32** | 31 |
| Median shot length | **1.8 s** | 1.8 s |
| First cut happens by | **1.9 s** | 2.2 s |

## Length depends on the format

| Format | n | Median length | Cuts/min | Talking |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Food montage | 7 | **16 s** | 40 | rarely |
| Customer reactions | 5 | **12 s** | 37 | never |
| Kitchen process | 6 | 40 s | 30 | sometimes (voiceover) |
| Creator / influencer | 13 | 51 s | 33 | always |
| Mixed | 8 | 49 s | 30 | mostly |

A wordless food montage wins at 12 to 20 seconds. Anything longer is carried by a voice telling a story.

## What the first words say (restaurant hooks)

The most common restaurant hook is **place + category claim + town**, set in big text over moving food:
- "BRAND NEW Chinese Restaurant in Austin, Texas"
- "Authentic Peking Duck in the Bay Area"
- "FIRST KOREAN BARBECUE AND HOT POT IN PANAMA CITY BEACH"
- "There's a brand new dim sum spot in the DMV"
- "MUST TRY thai + matcha in FAIRFAX, VA"
- "SAKURA RAMEN, Oakland Park, FL"

Next most common: a curiosity line ("What sound does a wok make?") and an offer ("ALL YOU CAN EAT").

## Offers that run long

Nearly every restaurant offer is a **specific price or value**: "All-you-can-eat dim sum from $38", "lunch $22.99", "kids under 5 free", "20% off first order with code". There are no vague "great deals".

## What this means for a small restaurant ad

1. Open on **motion with a hand in it**: chopsticks, a pour, a pull. Never a static room.
2. Put a **place + claim + town** line on screen in the first second. The page name already shows above the ad, so the video doesn't need a logo up front.
3. Keep a wordless food montage to **12 to 20 s**, with a cut about every **1.8 s**.
4. **Raw beats polished.** Phone-native footage outnumbers studio work 3 to 1.
5. Show **people**: hands at minimum, customers or staff when you can.
6. If there's an offer, make it a **specific number**.
7. End on the **best food shot** or a simple card, and say the action in plain words ("Order pickup", "Come hungry").
